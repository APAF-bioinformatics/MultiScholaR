## (a) Methods: simulation study of RUV designs

**Aim.** For each analysis route of the package, to measure whether the test that follows an RUV design holds its type I error rate and its false discovery rate, what the design gains or loses in power against no correction, and how it stands against other published methods. The study was planned and is reported as aims, data-generating mechanisms, targets, methods and performance measures, with Monte Carlo standard errors (Morris, White and Crowther 2019).

**Simulators (Figure S1a; Table S, simulator parameters).** Four simulators, one per analysis route, each generating data with known true effects and a known unwanted factor and then running the package's own code path for that route:

- Proteomics, classic: a log2 protein intensity matrix of 1,000 proteins (before the missing-value filter), with intensity-dependent missing values; analysed with the package's RUV-III-C correction or RUV-III factors and its limma test.
- Proteomics, DIA: precursor-level data from `limpa::simProteinDataSet` (1,000 proteins of 4 precursors each); the unwanted factor is added to the complete precursor matrix before missing values are drawn; quantified by the detection-probability-curve method and tested with `limpa::dpcDE`.
- Transcriptomics: negative binomial counts for 5,000 genes (before the expression filter); analysed with RUVSeq and the edgeR quasi-likelihood test.
- Lipidomics and metabolomics: 300 features in two assays of 150, with 10 internal standards (5 per assay), pooled QC injections (the first injection, after every 5 study injections, and the last) and drift along injection order; analysed per assay with limma. One class was simulated: the metabolomics code path gave identical output on one data set at start-up.

True effects are 1 log2. The unwanted factor has normal loadings whose variance is 3 times the median residual variance. In the lipidomics/metabolomics simulator the unwanted variation has two components at the centre (drift and one sample-wise factor). The correlation of the factor with the groups is exact by construction in three simulators and holds in expectation in the DIA simulator. The full parameter list, with the file that states each value, is `tableS-simulator-parameters.csv`.

**Scenarios (Figure S1a; Table S, scenario definitions).** A centre scenario: 2 groups, 6 biological units per group, 20% of features changed (half of them up), one unwanted factor uncorrelated with the groups, 25% of values missing where the omic has missing values, no technical replicates. Seven further main scenarios each change one setting: 12 units per group; 10% of features changed; no unwanted factor; no true effects (global null); factor correlated with the groups at 0.3; technical duplicates (4 units per group, 2 injections or libraries each); three groups. A second tier of 14 extra-hard scenarios (3 per group, 40% changed, correlation 0.6, three factors, no and 50% missing values, heavy-tailed noise, a known unbalanced batch, 80% of effects in one direction, loadings tied to abundance, and four combinations) states the conditions of use; by the pre-specified rule they never fail a design. Transcriptomics has no missing-value scenarios: its "no missing values" scenario is a low-count scenario (library size divided by 10) and "50% missing" was not run.

**Size and seeds.** 1,000 data sets per main scenario and design, 500 per extra-hard scenario and design. The seed of a data set is 100000 + 10000 x scenario index + data set number; the data are drawn once per data set and shared by all designs, so designs are compared on the same data. One attempt was made; no cell was re-run.

**Where the simulations ran, and equivalence of the two machines (decisions M13 and M19).** The main scenarios, and the extra-hard scenarios that finished there, ran on one workstation. The rest ran on GitHub-hosted runners with the same pinned package version and the same seeds: the extra-hard scenarios of the DIA simulator (all 14), six of classic proteomics and one of transcriptomics; the three designs added later (below); and the technical-duplicate scenario measured again (on the repaired package version). No extra-hard scenario was run on both machines and none mixes rows of the two (`kit-124/scored/final-20261118/inventory.tsv`; column `platform` of the results table). Before any runner result was placed beside the workstation's, the same data sets were analysed on both under a tolerance rule fixed beforehand (relative difference at most 1e-6 with an absolute floor of 1e-9; discrete values exact). Proof run, 20 data sets per scenario: no RUV, the ceiling, the recommended design and known null controls agreed in every scenario compared in all four simulators (1,120 rows), and every other design agreed except the legacy design (`kit-124/gha-proof-20261118/README.md` and its comparison files). In the run of the three further designs, no RUV and the ceiling were compared again on all 1,000 data sets of every main scenario: 96,000 rows, no discrete difference, and 8 rows of the transcriptomics simulator outside the tolerance on the bias of the estimated effects (and in one of them on the root mean square error), with relative differences of at most 6.7e-5; type I error rates, false discovery proportion, power, k and counts were identical in every row (`kit-124/scored/newarms-gha/compare/gate-summary.tsv`, `gate-rows-outside.tsv`). The gate as first declared (every value within the tolerance) was therefore not met in five transcriptomics scenarios. **This exception was accepted by the authors and is reported:** the gate is read on the values the decision rules use. The technical-duplicate scenario measured again has no workstation rows on the repaired version and was not compared.

**The legacy design is platform-dependent.** In the proof run the legacy design's results differed between the two machines in single data sets (classic proteomics, technical duplicates: 6 of 20 data sets; lipidomics/metabolomics: 5 and 3 of 20 in two main scenarios and 1 of 8 in an extra-hard one), by 1 to 4 calls per data set, with per-scenario means close (mean false discovery proportion 0.0369 against 0.0351, and 0.4134 against 0.4140). A second run on the runner with a reference linear-algebra library gave values that matched neither machine in the differing data sets; the cause was not established beyond sensitivity to rounding (`kit-124/gha-proof-20261118/README.md`, "The bounded BLAS test"). No other design moved. The legacy design's figures for the scenarios run on hosted runners (`legacy_platform_dependent` in the results table; double dagger in Figure S8) are therefore the runner's and would not be reproduced data set for data set on the workstation.

**Designs (Figure S1b; Table S, design definitions).** Each RUV design is a combination of four choices: the negative controls (all features; 50 features known to be null, standing for a supplied list, or the internal standards in lipidomics/metabolomics; the same list with true effects in it; features outside the 25% most significant of a first test, or with first-test q above 0.5; the least significant 1 to 30% of features with the share chosen by a sweep), the replicate sets (groups; technical replicates; none), the rule for the number of factors k (an estimated count; the package's separation rule; fixed at 1) and what the test reads (uncorrected data with the estimated factors in the model; the corrected data). Two references were run in every scenario: no RUV, and the true factor in the model (the ceiling). Other methods were run for comparison: RUV-4 and RUVinv with the known nulls, surrogate variable analysis, RUVr, pooled QC injections as the replicate set, internal-standard normalisation alone, QC-based LOESS drift correction, and the known batch in the model. The estimated count is `sva::num.sv(method = "be", B = 20)` with the group model, on the features complete in every sample; an estimate of 0 makes the design the no-RUV analysis of that data set. Designs with technical replicates as the replicate structure were run in the technical-duplicate scenario only. Three designs were added after the first scoring, in the main scenarios only, so that each selectable combination of control set and k rule has a measured figure: known null controls with the separation rule on those controls [design 22]; first-pass nulls with the estimated count [23]; and first-pass nulls with the separation rule computed on the first-pass controls [24; not in lipidomics/metabolomics, where the first-pass design already does this]. In the first scoring the known-null design [8] took the estimated count and the first-pass design [5] the legacy design's k (two proteomics routes, transcriptomics).

**The technical-duplicate scenario measured again.** In transcriptomics and lipidomics/metabolomics the simulated package version fitted technical replicates as independent samples and the scenario was void. After the package was repaired, the scenario was run again on the repaired version for no RUV, the ceiling and the recommended design (lipidomics/metabolomics: also the two designs with technical replicates as the replicate structure), 1,000 data sets. In transcriptomics the repaired package sums the two libraries of a unit before the analysis (8 samples); the designs with technical replicates cannot then be formed, and the ceiling's factor is the mean of a unit's two library scores, an approximate ceiling. These rows are reported beside the void originals, on another package version, and by the rule fixed before the confirming runs they judge no design.

**Calls.** A feature is called at q < 0.05 (`qvalue`, Benjamini-Hochberg where it cannot run; Benjamini-Hochberg in transcriptomics). In lipidomics/metabolomics q-values are computed per assay and contrast, and one false discovery proportion is scored per data set over both assays.

**Measures.** Per data set: the share of true null features with p < 0.05 and with p < 0.01 (type I error rate); the false discovery proportion among the called features (0 when nothing is called); power; bias and root mean square error of the log2 differences of the true effects. Per scenario and design: their means with Monte Carlo standard errors, the share of data sets with a false discovery proportion above 0.10, the share with any call, and the shares of refusals and crashes. A data set that a design refuses (by design, or by a comparator method's own stop) is scored with its no-RUV result; a crashed analysis has no measures and is counted.

**Decision rules (Figure S1b; Table S, decision rules).** Seven rules, the rule for void scenarios and the definition of pass, fail and inconclusive were fixed in writing, reviewed by a reader who did not write them, and revised before any confirming run; `tableS-decision-rules.csv` quotes them as declared, with their stated sources and the readings taken when they were applied. In short, with one-sided 97.5% bounds: (1) type I error rate at 0.05 at most 0.075 and at 0.01 at most 0.015; (2) mean false discovery proportion at most 0.075; (3) share of data sets with a false discovery proportion above 0.10 at most the ceiling's share plus 0.05; (4) where an unwanted factor exists, a gain over no RUV in power or in the share of rule 3; (5) where none exists, a power loss against no RUV of at most 0.02; (6) crashes at most 0.01; (7) under the global null, the share of data sets with any call at most 0.075. The limit 0.075 of rule 1 is the upper end of the band usually attributed to Bradley (1978), which was taken from secondary sources and applied one-sided; rules 2 to 7 are the study's own. A scenario in which the ceiling itself fails rule 1 or 2 is void and judges no design.

**Null check on public real data (Figure S9).** One public data set per omic: plasma of healthy volunteers (classic proteomics; Zenodo 17607826), single untreated HeLa cells in two culture batches (DIA; PXD024043), yeast wild-type replicates (transcriptomics; Schurch et al.) and urine of volunteers (metabolomics; MTBLS404). Per split, 12 biological units of one condition were drawn and put into two false groups of 6, so that every call is false; 100 distinct splits per kind of split, one attempt: random splits in all four, and in DIA and metabolomics also splits that align the recorded batch with the false groups at a correlation of 1/3. No RUV, the legacy design (in DIA in both of its forms) and the recommended design were run with the code of the simulators, on the simulated package version. Limits, data sets, seeds and the rule for a contradiction were written down before any run: mean share of features with p < 0.05 at most 0.075; share of splits with any call at most twice that of no RUV on the same splits, or at most 0.10; a design that "passed" in simulation and is above a limit, or that "failed" and is within both limits on every kind of split, is a contradiction. No bound is put on the means (the splits share samples). The check measures false calls, not power; one data set is one unit. Preparation is minimal and declared (`kit-125/declaration.md`); the DIA data set was run on a hosted runner after three lifecycle splits had reproduced the workstation's values.

Readings fixed while the rules were applied, and reported with the results (`tableS-decision-rules.csv`, column `reading_and_amendments`): the type I error rate is a mean over data sets with a normal bound, not a pooled binomial; rule 3's limit uses the ceiling's point share; rule 4 cannot be met in the global-null scenario by any design, the ceiling included, so verdicts were scored both literally and with that scenario judged without rule 4, and the choice was left open; "every main scenario" was read as every main scenario in which the design runs. Before the confirming runs, the technical-duplicate scenario was set aside as "reported, not used to judge" in transcriptomics and in lipidomics/metabolomics, where the package at the simulated version fitted technical replicates as independent samples.

**Software.** R 4.6.1 throughout. The package tree was pinned at one commit (0b6fcb6d) for all simulators; it includes two repairs made before the study (a bound on k in the classic sweep and the restoration of feature levels after correction), so "legacy" means the package after those repairs. Recorded package versions: DIA: MultiScholaR 0.5.0, limpa 1.4.2, fastlimpadpc 0.1.0, limma 3.68.4, ruv 0.9.7.1, sva 3.60.0, qvalue 2.44.0, statmod 1.5.2; transcriptomics: edgeR 4.10.1, limma 3.68.4, RUVSeq 1.46.0, EDASeq 2.46.0, sva 3.60.0, ruv 0.9.7.1; lipidomics/metabolomics: limma 3.68.4, qvalue 2.44.0, ruv 0.9.7.1, RUVIIIC 1.0.19, sva 3.60.0, statmod 1.5.2 (`tableS-simulator-parameters.csv`, rows `software`). **Gap:** the committed record of the classic-proteomics simulator holds its R version only; its package versions are in a run sidecar that is not committed and must be added before submission. The DIA route was run on the `fastlimpadpc` engine.

### The analysis method as offered in the software (decision record)

Source: `kit-124/design-review-20261118.md`, section 6, "Decision record for the Methods section" (rows M1 to M21). The rows are decisions of 2026-11-18 about the released software; several describe behaviour that differs from the package version that was simulated, and the text says so where it does. Numbers below carry their cell where they are in a committed table; a number that is in section 6 but in no committed table is marked "to be confirmed".

- **Option set (M1).** Every RUV design is offered in every omic, in tiers: recommended; valid with conditions; legacy, with a warning; none. Each option is shown with its measured false discovery proportion and a note of its conditions of use.
- **Default (M2).** Groups as pseudo-replicate sets, all features as negative controls, the estimated unwanted factors in the model of the test; pre-selected in all four omics.

  **To be stated with the default, prominently.** By the study's pre-specified rules this design did not pass outright in every omic. Read literally, no RUV design passed in any omic, because rule 4 cannot be met under the global null by any design, the ceiling included; the literal outcome of the rule was "RUV off by default" in classic proteomics, transcriptomics and lipidomics/metabolomics and "neither passes" in DIA (`kit-124/outcome.md`, "What the rule gives, literally"). With the global-null scenario judged without rule 4, the recommended design passed in DIA, was inconclusive in transcriptomics, and failed in classic proteomics (rule 3, the share of data sets above 0.10, at 12 per group) and in lipidomics/metabolomics (rule 3 at 12 per group and with the factor tied to the groups) (`kit-124/scored/s<i>-core/k124-arms.tsv`, arm 7, columns `verdict` and `verdict_without_rule4_in_C4`). The authors chose it as the default because it was the best-calibrated design measured: its mean false discovery proportion stayed within the 0.075 limit in every judged main scenario of every omic and at or near the ceiling's (Results 2), without needing a control list, and its number of factors is estimated rather than fixed (fixing it at 1 also stayed within the limit in the main scenarios, and lost all power with three factors: Results 8). That is a decision of the authors on the measurements, not a verdict of the pre-specified rule.

  **Its measured limits (conditions of use; Results 8, Table S conditions of use).** Classic proteomics: outside no limit in 13 judged extra-hard scenarios, inconclusive on the tail in 4. DIA: outside a limit in 5 of 13 (3 per group, alone and combined; three factors; the factor tied to the groups at 0.6). Transcriptomics: outside in 1 of 11 (3 per group with three factors, where it makes no calls). Lipidomics/metabolomics: outside in 3 of 10 (factor tied at 0.6; a known unbalanced batch; 50% missing, on the tail only), and not measurable at 3 per group. Main scenarios: the share of single data sets above 0.10 is high in lipidomics/metabolomics under every analysis, and above the ceiling's in classic proteomics at 12 per group (Results 6). On real data it was within both limits in all four data sets checked (Results 9).
- **Legacy (M3).** The earlier design is kept unchanged as an option so that earlier analyses can be reproduced. It is not the default because its mean false discovery proportion at a nominal 0.05 reached 0.299 to 0.556 in its worst judged main scenario, depending on the omic ([S3, C5, 3] and [S4, C2, 3]; Results 1).
- **DIA with limpa (M4).** The default replaces the control rule of the limpa workflow script (the least significant 1 to 30% of proteins, the share chosen by a sweep), which stays available as the legacy option. With the factors in the model and the factor correlated with the groups, the sweep's controls gave 0.435 [S2, C5, 4]; all features as controls gave at most 0.055 in any judged main scenario with true effects [S2, C6, 7]. The figures were measured on the `fastlimpadpc` engine, not on `limpa`.
- **Number of unwanted factors (M5, revised by M15).** The estimated count (`sva::num.sv`, method "be", 20 permutations, seeded) is the pre-selected rule for every option where it is defined, first-pass nulls included (M15 revises M5 for that option, on the designs scored later: Results 5a); the package's separation rule stays selectable where a separate control set exists, with its measured figures shown; a number set by the user always possible; a diagnostic plot for either rule. Provenance as in section (c): the estimated count is the surrogate-variable-analysis convention applied to RUV, with applied precedent only; the separation rule is the package's own heuristic. Which rule each design's figures were measured with is in `tableS-design-definitions.csv` (column `number_of_factors`).
- **Technical replicates (M6).** Where a design declares technical replicates they are pre-selected as the replicate structure. In transcriptomics technical replicates are summed before analysis (the decision record cites the edgeR User's Guide, section 4.9.1), so the option is shown disabled there with the reason. A design with one unit per group is refused in every omic. Note for the text: this is the behaviour after a repair; the simulated package version fitted technical replicates as independent samples in transcriptomics and lipidomics/metabolomics, which is why that scenario is void there; it was measured again on the repaired version (Results 5b).
- **First-pass null controls (M7).** Offered in every omic with their conditions of use, including DIA and lipidomics/metabolomics where they measured poorly with the factor correlated with the groups (0.149 [S2, C5, 5]; 0.239 [S4, C5, 5]). With the estimated count, now the pre-selected rule for this option, the same cells are 0.146 [S2, C5, 23] and 0.174 [S4, C5, 23] (Results 5a).
- **Level shift in lipidomics and metabolomics (M8).** The shift of feature levels after RUV-III correction is repaired in every option, the legacy option included, so in these two omics the legacy option does not reproduce earlier releases bit for bit: the corrected table differs by a constant per feature, and contrasts are unchanged on complete data. To be noted in the release notes, and in the paper only if earlier results are compared. No committed table of this supplement measures it.
- **Features missing in a whole group (M9).** A feature not observed in one group is still tested in the comparisons between the groups where it is observed, and is flagged as not observed in that group; it is not dropped from every comparison. This follows limma's default handling of missing values. It departs from the simulators, whose kernel dropped such features (most simulated scenarios had two groups), so the simulation figures do not cover it.
- **What is tested and what is displayed (M10).** Under the designs with the factors in the model, the test reads the uncorrected (normalised) data; displayed and exported intensities, heatmaps, PCA and the input to integration are the RUV-corrected matrix. Fold changes and p-values come from the model, so a figure drawn from the corrected matrix need not reproduce them exactly (difference "up to 0.18 log2 units in the audit's data": to be confirmed from the final tables; no committed table holds it). The basis is the RUVSeq vignette and the limma documentation quoted in section (c). The alternative of displaying the data minus the fitted factor terms was considered and not taken.
- **Warning when an estimated factor follows the groups (M11).** A warning is to be shown when an estimated unwanted factor is associated with the groups; its threshold is to be set on one set of simulated data and accepted on another, by a rule fixed beforehand (the warning fires in at most 10% of unrelated data sets and in at least 80% of related ones; both figures are the declared rule, to be confirmed from the final tables together with the threshold). If no threshold meets the rule, the value is shown with no verdict. **Not yet statable:** the threshold is not set.
- **Software and platform (M12, M13, M19).** `fastduckdb` is a required dependency of the package and of every installer (without it the intensity filter of lipidomics/metabolomics is refused); its availability has to be stated with the software requirements. Where the simulations ran and how the two machines were shown to agree, with the accepted exception, is in "Where the simulations ran" above.
- **Proteins tested in classic proteomics (M14).** Under the designs with the factors in the model, the test reads every protein of the normalised matrix (the population the simulations tested), not only the proteins that survive RUV-III-C and the missing-value clean-up of the corrected matrix. A protein tested without a corrected value is flagged and has no displayed intensity, so the tested set and the displayed set can differ. The counts in the decision record (786 against 776 proteins in the centre scenario; 710 against 625 at 3 per group with three factors) are from an audit and in no table of this supplement: to be confirmed.
- **A further simulation round before release (M16). Not yet measured.** Four changes found by audits of the analysis code are to be measured, each as its own design and all together, before they ship: (a) exact per-feature standard errors for comparisons when features have missing values and the model carries the unwanted factors; (b) the proteomics test applying the variance trend and robust settings it sets; (c) on designs with declared technical replicates, the estimated count removing the biological units and not only the groups; (d) 200 permutations for the estimated count instead of 20. The Methods can describe the final method only with the figures measured on it; the figures in this supplement are for the method as simulated. The sizes quoted in the decision record for each (standard error ratio 0.88 to 2.96; p differing by up to 0.004 and 0.17; a seed-dependent verdict in 19% of no-factor data sets at 20 permutations and 4% at 200) are from the audits and in no table of this supplement: to be confirmed.
- **Cyclic loess normalisation (M17).** Refused below 10 features per assay in lipidomics and metabolomics, with a message recommending another method. The minimum is a judgement about a meaningful fit, not a measurement (the fit was found unstable with 2 or 3 features; decision record). Not part of the simulations.
- **Windows path length (M18).** Staging folder names for saved figures are shortened and figures are rendered in a short temporary folder; a figure that cannot be written stops the step with a message. A software requirement, unconfirmed on a Windows machine at the time of the record. Not part of the simulations.
- **Availability of the dependency (M20).** `fast_duckdb` is to be made public (or the upload rule changed) at the public build; to be stated with the software's availability at publication.
- **The real-data contradiction (M21).** The real-data check contradicts the simulations in one row, by the rule declared beforehand: no RUV in DIA (Results 9). It was recorded and accepted by the authors as not bearing on the default, since the recommended design is consistent on real data in all four omics and the legacy design is outside the limits in all four. To be stated as a limit of the simulations: the simulated unwanted variation is stronger than that of the one real data set in which it was measured, and "no RUV" can be adequate when unwanted variation is weak.

**Figures and tables** are written by one script (`figures.R`) from the committed scored tables, the per-data-set table, the aggregate tables of the real-data check and one definitions file; intervals drawn are means ± 1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors, and exact one-sided 97.5% bounds for shares.

## (b) Results: one paragraph per finding

**1. The legacy design does not hold its error rates.** In its worst judged main scenario with true effects the legacy design's mean false discovery proportion at a nominal 0.05 was 0.385 in classic proteomics [S1, C5, 3], 0.466 in DIA [S2, C5, 3], 0.299 in transcriptomics [S3, C5, 3] and 0.556 in lipidomics/metabolomics [S4, C2, 3] (Figure S2; Figure S4, open points). Its type I error rate in the same cells was 0.194, 0.287, 0.179 and 0.161 (Figure S3), so the excess is in the test and not in the multiplicity adjustment. In the centre scenario the values were 0.098 [S1, C0, 3], 0.331 [S2, C0, 3], 0.047 [S3, C0, 3] and 0.470 [S4, C0, 3]: in transcriptomics the legacy design was at the nominal level except when the factor was correlated with the groups [S3, C5, 3], while in DIA and lipidomics/metabolomics it failed rules 1 to 3 in nearly every judged main scenario (columns `rule1` to `rule3`). Its power in the centre scenario (0.920, 0.911, 0.729, 0.423 [S1 to S4, C0, 3]) was close to the ceiling's (0.914, 0.905, 0.774, 0.498 [S1 to S4, C0, 2]).

**2. All features as controls, with the factors in the model, matched the ceiling on average.** Over the judged main scenarios with true effects the recommended design's mean false discovery proportion ranged from 0.052 to 0.066 in classic proteomics [S1, C3 and C1, 7], 0.049 to 0.055 in DIA [S2, C3 and C6, 7], 0.044 to 0.055 in transcriptomics [S3, C3 and C2, 7] and 0.057 to 0.072 in lipidomics/metabolomics [S4, C0 and C5, 7]; the ceiling's ranges were 0.050 to 0.052 [S1, C1 and C2, 2], 0.047 to 0.055 [S2, C2 and C6, 2], 0.044 to 0.055 [S3, C3 and C2, 2] and 0.047 to 0.061 [S4, C1 and C2, 2] (Figure S2). Power in the centre scenario was 0.914, 0.909, 0.774 and 0.434 [S1 to S4, C0, 7] against the ceiling's 0.914, 0.905, 0.774 and 0.498 (Figure S4). Under the global null the share of data sets with any call was 0.061 [S1, C4, 7], 0.044 [S2, C4, 7] and 0.074 [S3, C4, 7], against 0.061, 0.051 and 0.075 for the ceiling [S1 to S3, C4, 2]; lipidomics/metabolomics has no global-null check (Limits). By the pre-specified rules the design passed every judged main scenario in DIA except the global null, where it was inconclusive [S2, C4, 7, `verdict`]; it failed rule 3 at 12 per group in classic proteomics [S1, C1, 7] and in two lipidomics/metabolomics scenarios [S4, C1 and C5, 7], with three more inconclusive on the same rule [S4, C0, C2 and C7, 7] (finding 6); and in classic proteomics and transcriptomics its only other failing cell was the global null, on rule 4, which the ceiling also fails there [S1 and S3, C4, 2 and 7, `rule4`]. The design therefore did not pass outright in every omic by the pre-specified rules: with the global null judged without rule 4 its verdict is pass in DIA, inconclusive in transcriptomics and fail in classic proteomics and lipidomics/metabolomics, on rule 3 only (`k124-arms.tsv`, arm 7). It was chosen as the default by the authors as the best-calibrated design measured (Methods, Default).

**3. ### Limits (to state in the text)

- **The default did not pass the pre-specified rules outright in every omic.** It was chosen by the authors as the best-calibrated design measured (Methods, Default; Results 2). Its conditions of use are measured, not guaranteed: Results 8.
- **Simulation, with one real-data check of false calls.** The simulators generate the linear model that RUV assumes: a failure under it is strong evidence against a design, a pass is weaker evidence for it. The real-data check covers four data sets, one per omic, on null splits; it measures false calls, not power, and nothing in it says the recommended design finds more. It ran the simulators' code on the simulated package version, not the released analysis code.
- **The simulated unwanted variation is stronger than in the one real data set where it was measured,** and the real-data check contradicts the simulation for no RUV in DIA (Results 9). The simulations may overstate the harm of not correcting when unwanted variation is weak.
- **Options not checked on real data:** known null controls, first-pass nulls and technical replicates as the replicate structure were not run on real data; nor were four further workflows for which data sets had been accepted (DIA with the classic quantification, LFQ with MaxQuant, TMT, lipidomics) (`kit-125/outcome.md`, "Not checked on real data").
- **Technical replicates were judged in one scenario,** in the two proteomics routes. In transcriptomics and lipidomics/metabolomics that scenario was void on the simulated version; measured again on the repaired version it is calibrated for the designs run (Results 5b), on a hosted runner without a comparison against the workstation, and it judges no design.
- **Lipidomics/metabolomics has no global-null check.** One false discovery proportion is scored per data set over two assays that each control their own false discovery rate, so the ceiling's mean is 0.130 under the global null [S4, C4, 2] and the scenario is void. Its four scenarios with 3 per group are void too, so nothing can be said about 3 per group in these omics.
- **Scenarios that could not fault a design:** besides those, heavy tails and one-directional effects in transcriptomics, 3 per group with three factors in classic proteomics, and 50% missing without a factor in DIA.
- **One DIA engine.** The DIA route was measured on the `fastlimpadpc` engine; the result is expected, not measured, to hold for the `limpa` engine. On complete data the route failed in half the data sets (Results 8).
- **Two machines.** Part of the results ran on hosted runners; equivalence rests on the checks in the Methods, with one accepted exception (8 rows, bias and root mean square error only), and no extra-hard scenario exists on both machines. The legacy design's values are platform-dependent.
- **The designs scored later were run in the main scenarios only;** first-pass nulls with the estimated count, the pre-selected form of that option, have no extra-hard figure.
- **The global-null scenario cannot be passed on rule 4** by any design; verdicts are given under both readings.
- **S6 and S7 cover the main scenarios of the first scoring only** (the per-data-set results of the later runs are not committed).
- **The method as released will differ from the method as simulated** in the four points of decision M16, which are not yet measured.

## (c) Relation to the method papers

Only sources that the design review (`kit-124/design-review-20261118.md`) records as read are cited in this section; quotes are as recorded there.

The computations of the package equal those of the reference packages (reference-match tests, not part of this supplement). What differs between designs is how RUV is used.

- **Groups as replicate sets** follow published usage: the RUVSeq vignette's RUVs example forms the replicate sets from the treatment groups (`differences <- makeGroups(x)`), Molania et al. (2019) describe "an approach using pseudo-replicates when technical replicates are not available", and Salim et al. (2022) allow that "cells with the same level of the biological factor of interest can be declared to be a pseudo-replicate set". The legacy design uses the same replicate sets; this is not one of its departures.
- **All features as controls (recommended design)** follows the RUVSeq vignette for replicate-based RUV: "RUVs is robust to that choice and ... using all the genes works well in practice". Molania et al. (2019) put it as an open question for RUV-III ("Does using all genes as negative controls help? We will see examples where doing so does help, and others where it does not"), and Kim et al. (2021) found "no significant differences" between data-driven non-changing controls and all metabolites as controls. A-priori known controls remain the first choice in these papers; the recommended design is the documented fallback when none exist.
- **Factors in the model of the test (recommended and conditional designs)** follow the RUVSeq vignette ("It is important that subsequent DE analysis be done on the original counts ... as removing the unwanted factors from the counts can also remove part of a factor of interest") and the limma documentation for `removeBatchEffect` ("This function is not intended to be used prior to linear modelling"); Nygaard, Rødland and Hovig (2016) show that correcting and then testing "may lead to exaggerated confidence in downstream analyses".
- **A supplied control list** is the usage of the RUV-III-C paper (Poulos et al. 2020: negative controls known a priori) and of Salim et al. (2022: housekeeping genes).
- **First-pass null controls with most features kept** follow the RUVSeq vignette's RUVg example ("least significantly DE genes based on a first-pass DE analysis"; its code keeps all but the top 5,000 genes).
- **Technical replicates as the replicate structure** are the usage of Molania et al. (2019) and Poulos et al. (2020).

**Where the legacy design departs.** (1) Controls: the least significant 1 to 30% of features of a first test, with the share chosen by a sweep; the papers above use either all features, a list known in advance, or a wide first-pass set. (2) k: chosen by the same sweep. (3) The test: in classic proteomics, lipidomics and metabolomics it is run on the corrected data. Departure (3) is itself a published usage: Molania et al. (2019) and Jiang, Gagnon-Bartsch and Speed (arXiv 2310.01918) run limma on the RUV-III-adjusted matrix, the latter stating that "the asymptotic theory developed in this paper does not address the validity of the p-values". The simulations isolate (1) with (2) and (3) separately (finding 4).

**The two rules for k.** The RUV method papers give no rule and ask for judgement. Gagnon-Bartsch and Speed (2012): "there is no clear way to determine k. We recommend pursuing several approaches and exercising judgment."; Gagnon-Bartsch, Jacob and Speed (2013, technical report): "We are unaware of any good algorithmic way to estimate k, and we feel there is an important role for human judgment."; Risso et al. (2014): "The choice of k should be guided by considerations that include sample size, extent of technical effects captured by the first k factors, and extent of differential expression"; Jacob, Gagnon-Bartsch and Speed (2016): "No single rule can be therefore given as to hyperparameter choice and judgment is necessary each time adjustments are performed"; Molania et al. (2023): "RUV-III is generally robust to overestimating K but not always." The RUVSeq vignette's example uses k = 1.

The *estimated count* used here is the permutation estimate of the number of surrogate variables (`sva::num.sv`, method "be", which its help page credits to Buja and Eyuboglu 1992; the procedure is step 4 of the algorithm of Leek and Storey 2007, with the threshold fixed at 0.10 and 20 permutations by the function). It is the convention of surrogate variable analysis applied to RUV. Its precedent is applied use only: four applied papers were found that set k for RUVSeq with `num.sv` (Gatta et al. 2021; Bingen et al. 2022; Marquez et al. 2025; Munzir et al. 2026), from two overlapping author groups, none giving a methodological reason. No RUV method paper proposes it, and the RUV-2 authors state a reservation about counting factors by testing ("Using hypothesis testing to find k does not account for this bias-variance trade-off"; of SVA's approach: "we feel there are problems with this approach"). Its support in this work is the simulation (finding 7), not a method paper. The *separation rule* (the k at which the canonical-correlation curve of all features and that of the controls are furthest apart, on the plot of `ruv::ruv_cancorplot`, within a bound set by sample size) is the package's own heuristic: no rule for reading k off that plot was found in the `ruv` package, in the 2013 technical report, in Jiang et al. or by a full-text search, and it is undefined when all features are the controls. Both statements should stand in the paper as plainly as here. The user-chosen k and the diagnostic plots that the software keeps are what the method papers themselves ask for.
