## (a) Methods: simulation study of RUV designs

**Aim.** For each analysis route of the package, to measure whether the test that follows an RUV design holds its type I error rate and its false discovery rate, what the design gains or loses in power against no correction, and how it stands against other published methods. The study was planned and is reported as aims, data-generating mechanisms, targets, methods and performance measures, with Monte Carlo standard errors (Morris, White and Crowther 2019).

**Simulators (Figure S1a; Table S, simulator parameters).** Four simulators, one per analysis route, each generating data with known true effects and a known unwanted factor and then running the package's own code path for that route:

- Proteomics, classic: a log2 protein intensity matrix of 1,000 proteins (before the missing-value filter), with intensity-dependent missing values; analysed with the package's RUV-III-C correction or RUV-III factors and its limma test.
- Proteomics, DIA: precursor-level data from `limpa::simProteinDataSet` (1,000 proteins of 4 precursors each); the unwanted factor is added to the complete precursor matrix before missing values are drawn; quantified by the detection-probability-curve method and tested with `limpa::dpcDE`.
- Transcriptomics: negative binomial counts for 5,000 genes (before the expression filter); analysed with RUVSeq and the edgeR quasi-likelihood test.
- Lipidomics and metabolomics: 300 features in two assays of 150, with 10 internal standards (5 per assay), pooled QC injections (the first injection, after every 5 study injections, and the last) and drift along injection order; analysed per assay with limma. One class was simulated: the metabolomics code path gave identical output on one data set at start-up.

True effects are 1 log2. The unwanted factor has normal loadings whose variance is 3 times the median residual variance. In the lipidomics/metabolomics simulator the unwanted variation has two components at the centre (drift and one sample-wise factor). The correlation of the factor with the groups is exact by construction in three simulators and holds in expectation in the DIA simulator. The full parameter list, with the file that states each value, is `tableS-simulator-parameters.csv`.

**Scenarios (Figure S1a; Table S, scenario definitions).** A centre scenario: 2 groups, 6 biological units per group, 20% of features changed (half of them up), one unwanted factor uncorrelated with the groups, 25% of values missing where the omic has missing values, no technical replicates. Seven further main scenarios each change one setting: 12 units per group; 10% of features changed; no unwanted factor; no true effects (global null); factor correlated with the groups at 0.3; technical duplicates (4 units per group, 2 injections or libraries each); three groups. A second tier of 14 extra-hard scenarios (3 per group, 40% changed, correlation 0.6, three factors, no and 50% missing values, heavy-tailed noise, a known unbalanced batch, 80% of effects in one direction, loadings tied to abundance, and four combinations) states the conditions of use; by the pre-specified rule they never fail a design. Transcriptomics has no missing-value scenarios: its "no missing values" scenario is a low-count scenario (library size divided by 10) and "50% missing" was not run.

**Size and seeds.** 1,000 data sets per main scenario and design, 500 per extra-hard scenario and design. The seed of a data set is 100000 + 10000 x scenario index + data set number; the data are drawn once per data set and shared by all designs, so designs are compared on the same data. One attempt was made; no cell was re-run.

**Designs (Figure S1b; Table S, design definitions).** Each RUV design is a combination of four choices: the negative controls (all features; 50 features known to be null, standing for a supplied list, or the internal standards in lipidomics/metabolomics; the same list with true effects in it; features outside the 25% most significant of a first test, or with first-test q above 0.5; the least significant 1 to 30% of features with the share chosen by a sweep), the replicate sets (groups; technical replicates; none), the rule for the number of factors k (an estimated count; the package's separation rule; fixed at 1) and what the test reads (uncorrected data with the estimated factors in the model; the corrected data). Two references were run in every scenario: no RUV, and the true factor in the model (the ceiling). Other methods were run for comparison: RUV-4 and RUVinv with the known nulls, surrogate variable analysis, RUVr, pooled QC injections as the replicate set, internal-standard normalisation alone, QC-based LOESS drift correction, and the known batch in the model. The estimated count is `sva::num.sv(method = "be", B = 20)` with the group model, on the features complete in every sample; an estimate of 0 makes the design the no-RUV analysis of that data set. Designs with technical replicates as the replicate structure were run in the technical-duplicate scenario only.

**Calls.** A feature is called at q < 0.05 (`qvalue`, Benjamini-Hochberg where it cannot run; Benjamini-Hochberg in transcriptomics). In lipidomics/metabolomics q-values are computed per assay and contrast, and one false discovery proportion is scored per data set over both assays.

**Measures.** Per data set: the share of true null features with p < 0.05 and with p < 0.01 (type I error rate); the false discovery proportion among the called features (0 when nothing is called); power; bias and root mean square error of the log2 differences of the true effects. Per scenario and design: their means with Monte Carlo standard errors, the share of data sets with a false discovery proportion above 0.10, the share with any call, and the shares of refusals and crashes. A data set that a design refuses (by design, or by a comparator method's own stop) is scored with its no-RUV result; a crashed analysis has no measures and is counted.

**Decision rules (Figure S1b; Table S, decision rules).** Seven rules, the rule for void scenarios and the definition of pass, fail and inconclusive were fixed in writing, reviewed by a reader who did not write them, and revised before any confirming run; `tableS-decision-rules.csv` quotes them as declared, with their stated sources and the readings taken when they were applied. In short, with one-sided 97.5% bounds: (1) type I error rate at 0.05 at most 0.075 and at 0.01 at most 0.015; (2) mean false discovery proportion at most 0.075; (3) share of data sets with a false discovery proportion above 0.10 at most the ceiling's share plus 0.05; (4) where an unwanted factor exists, a gain over no RUV in power or in the share of rule 3; (5) where none exists, a power loss against no RUV of at most 0.02; (6) crashes at most 0.01; (7) under the global null, the share of data sets with any call at most 0.075. The limit 0.075 of rule 1 is the upper end of the band usually attributed to Bradley (1978), which was taken from secondary sources and applied one-sided; rules 2 to 7 are the study's own. A scenario in which the ceiling itself fails rule 1 or 2 is void and judges no design.

Readings fixed while the rules were applied, and reported with the results (`tableS-decision-rules.csv`, column `reading_and_amendments`): the type I error rate is a mean over data sets with a normal bound, not a pooled binomial; rule 3's limit uses the ceiling's point share; rule 4 cannot be met in the global-null scenario by any design, the ceiling included, so verdicts were scored both literally and with that scenario judged without rule 4, and the choice was left open; "every main scenario" was read as every main scenario in which the design runs. Before the confirming runs, the technical-duplicate scenario was set aside as "reported, not used to judge" in transcriptomics and in lipidomics/metabolomics, where the package at the simulated version fitted technical replicates as independent samples.

**Software.** R 4.6.1 throughout. The package tree was pinned at one commit (0b6fcb6d) for all simulators; it includes two repairs made before the study (a bound on k in the classic sweep and the restoration of feature levels after correction), so "legacy" means the package after those repairs. Recorded package versions: DIA: MultiScholaR 0.5.0, limpa 1.4.2, fastlimpadpc 0.1.0, limma 3.68.4, ruv 0.9.7.1, sva 3.60.0, qvalue 2.44.0, statmod 1.5.2; transcriptomics: edgeR 4.10.1, limma 3.68.4, RUVSeq 1.46.0, EDASeq 2.46.0, sva 3.60.0, ruv 0.9.7.1; lipidomics/metabolomics: limma 3.68.4, qvalue 2.44.0, ruv 0.9.7.1, RUVIIIC 1.0.19, sva 3.60.0, statmod 1.5.2 (`tableS-simulator-parameters.csv`, rows `software`). **Gap:** the committed record of the classic-proteomics simulator holds its R version only; its package versions are in a run sidecar that is not committed and must be added before submission. The DIA route was run on the `fastlimpadpc` engine.

**Figures and tables** are written by one script (`figures.R`) from the committed scored tables, the per-data-set table and one definitions file; intervals drawn are means ± 1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors, and exact one-sided 97.5% bounds for shares.

## (b) Results: one paragraph per finding

**1. The legacy design does not hold its error rates.** In its worst judged main scenario with true effects the legacy design's mean false discovery proportion at a nominal 0.05 was 0.385 in classic proteomics [S1, C5, 3], 0.466 in DIA [S2, C5, 3], 0.299 in transcriptomics [S3, C5, 3] and 0.556 in lipidomics/metabolomics [S4, C2, 3] (Figure S2; Figure S4, open points). Its type I error rate in the same cells was 0.194, 0.287, 0.179 and 0.161 (Figure S3), so the excess is in the test and not in the multiplicity adjustment. In the centre scenario the values were 0.098 [S1, C0, 3], 0.331 [S2, C0, 3], 0.047 [S3, C0, 3] and 0.470 [S4, C0, 3]: in transcriptomics the legacy design was at the nominal level except when the factor was correlated with the groups [S3, C5, 3], while in DIA and lipidomics/metabolomics it failed rules 1 to 3 in nearly every judged main scenario (columns `rule1` to `rule3`). Its power in the centre scenario (0.920, 0.911, 0.729, 0.423 [S1 to S4, C0, 3]) was close to the ceiling's (0.914, 0.905, 0.774, 0.498 [S1 to S4, C0, 2]).

**2. All features as controls, with the factors in the model, matched the ceiling on average.** Over the judged main scenarios with true effects the recommended design's mean false discovery proportion ranged from 0.052 to 0.066 in classic proteomics [S1, C3 and C1, 7], 0.049 to 0.055 in DIA [S2, C3 and C6, 7], 0.044 to 0.055 in transcriptomics [S3, C3 and C2, 7] and 0.057 to 0.072 in lipidomics/metabolomics [S4, C0 and C5, 7]; the ceiling's ranges were 0.050 to 0.052 [S1, C1 and C2, 2], 0.047 to 0.055 [S2, C2 and C6, 2], 0.044 to 0.055 [S3, C3 and C2, 2] and 0.047 to 0.061 [S4, C1 and C2, 2] (Figure S2). Power in the centre scenario was 0.914, 0.909, 0.774 and 0.434 [S1 to S4, C0, 7] against the ceiling's 0.914, 0.905, 0.774 and 0.498 (Figure S4). Under the global null the share of data sets with any call was 0.061 [S1, C4, 7], 0.044 [S2, C4, 7] and 0.074 [S3, C4, 7], against 0.061, 0.051 and 0.075 for the ceiling [S1 to S3, C4, 2]; lipidomics/metabolomics has no global-null check (Limits). By the pre-specified rules the design passed every judged main scenario in DIA except the global null, where it was inconclusive [S2, C4, 7, `verdict`]; it failed rule 3 at 12 per group in classic proteomics [S1, C1, 7] and in two lipidomics/metabolomics scenarios [S4, C1 and C5, 7], with three more inconclusive on the same rule [S4, C0, C2 and C7, 7] (finding 6); and in classic proteomics and transcriptomics its only other failing cell was the global null, on rule 4, which the ceiling also fails there [S1 and S3, C4, 2 and 7, `rule4`].

**3. No correction is not a neutral fallback.** With an unwanted factor present, no RUV was conservative in three omics (type I error rate 0.014 [S1, C0, 1], 0.010 [S3, C0, 1], 0.012 [S4, C0, 1]) and lost most of its power: 0.493 against 0.914 [S1, C0, 1 and 7], 0.005 against 0.774 [S3, C0, 1 and 7], 0.003 against 0.434 [S4, C0, 1 and 7] (Figure S4). In DIA it was not calibrated: with the factor correlated with the groups its type I error rate was 0.116 and its mean false discovery proportion 0.191 [S2, C5, 1], and in the centre scenario 14.9% of data sets had a false discovery proportion above 0.10 [S2, C0, 1, `fdp10_share`].

**4. Each of the legacy design's two departures costs calibration by itself (Figure S5).** Test form, with the same known-null controls and the same k: with the factors in the model the mean false discovery proportion in the centre scenario was 0.051, 0.051, 0.048 and 0.050 [S1 to S4, C0, 8]; with the test run on the corrected data it was 0.089, 0.104, 0.061 and 0.180 [S1 to S4, C0, 10]. Control set, with the factors in the model in both: when the factor was correlated with the groups, all features as controls gave 0.056, 0.053 and 0.072 [S1, S2, S4, C5, 7] and the legacy control set with its k gave 0.322, 0.435 and 0.153 [S1, S2, S4, C5, 4]; in transcriptomics, where the legacy design already has the factors in the model, the same comparison is 0.048 [S3, C5, 7] against 0.299 [S3, C5, 3]. In DIA the legacy control set was liberal in the centre scenario as well (0.290 [S2, C0, 4]); in classic proteomics it was not (0.047 [S1, C0, 4]). The two control sets also differ in the k rule, which goes with the control set.

**5. The conditional options.** Known null controls with the factors in the model stayed near the nominal level in every judged main scenario with true effects (mean false discovery proportion 0.049 to 0.053 in classic proteomics, 0.048 to 0.056 in DIA, 0.043 to 0.056 in transcriptomics, 0.041 to 0.068 in lipidomics/metabolomics [S1 to S4, C0 to C7 without the void cells, 8]); in lipidomics/metabolomics, where the controls are the 10 internal standards, power in the centre scenario was 0.366 against 0.434 for all features as controls [S4, C0, 8 and 7], and in transcriptomics the design lost power where no unwanted factor existed (0.696 against 0.782 without RUV [S3, C3, 8 and 1]; it fails rule 5 there). First-pass null controls (the 25% most significant dropped) were near nominal in the centre scenario in classic proteomics and transcriptomics (0.052 [S1, C0, 5]; 0.047 [S3, C0, 5]) but not in DIA or lipidomics/metabolomics (0.094 [S2, C0, 5]; 0.097 [S4, C0, 5]), rose when the factor was correlated with the groups (0.078, 0.149, 0.064, 0.239 [S1 to S4, C5, 5]), and lost power in transcriptomics (0.601 against 0.774 [S3, C0, 5 and 7]). Technical replicates as the replicate structure were judged in the technical-duplicate scenario of the two proteomics routes only: in classic proteomics the mean false discovery proportion was 0.054 with the factors in the model and 0.047 with the corrected data tested, power 0.896 and 0.871 [S1, C6, 16 and 15], against 0.054 and 0.896 for the recommended design in the same scenario [S1, C6, 7]; in DIA, 0.058 and power 0.756 with the corrected data tested [S2, C6, 15] against 0.055 and 0.768 [S2, C6, 7] (Figures S2 and S3).

**6. An average at the nominal level is not every data set at the nominal level (Figure S6).** In the two proteomics routes and in transcriptomics the false discovery proportion of single data sets under the recommended design was concentrated near 0.05: its 99th percentile in the centre scenario was 0.107, 0.095 and 0.065 (per run [S1 to S3, C0, 7]; ceiling 0.102, 0.092, 0.065 [S1 to S3, C0, 2]). Two qualifiers. In lipidomics/metabolomics the share of data sets with a false discovery proportion above 0.10 was 0.153 to 0.278 over the judged main scenarios with true effects [S4, C3 and C2, 7, `fdp10_share`], and 0.075 to 0.255 for the ceiling [S4, C1 and C2, 2]: the spread is wide under every analysis, the ceiling included, in a simulator with 300 features in which 7 to 51% of data sets have a proportion of exactly 0 (per run [S4, C7 and C2, 7]). In classic proteomics at 12 per group, 11.5% of data sets exceeded 0.10 under the recommended design against 0.7% under the ceiling [S1, C1, 7 and 2, `fdp10_share`]; its mean false discovery proportion there was 0.066 and its type I error rate 0.058 [S1, C1, 7], so the design is somewhat liberal in that scenario, not only more variable.

**7. The number of unwanted factors (Figure S7).** Where a factor existed, the estimated count equalled the true number in 99.4 to 100% of data sets in the two proteomics routes and in transcriptomics, and in 92.7 to 99.9% in lipidomics/metabolomics (per run [S1 to S4, main scenarios other than C3, 7]). Without an unwanted factor it returned 0 in 88.9%, 86.7% and 77.2% of data sets in classic proteomics, DIA and lipidomics/metabolomics, but in only 36.2% in transcriptomics (per run [S1, S2, S4, S3; C3; 7]; mean k 1.355 [S3, C3, 7, `mean_k`]). In that transcriptomics scenario the over-estimate did not show in the error rate or in power: mean false discovery proportion 0.044 and power 0.776 [S3, C3, 7] against 0.044 and 0.782 without RUV [S3, C3, 1]. The separation rule returned more than the true number in 21.7 to 100% of data sets depending on omic and scenario and never returned 0 (per run [S1 to S4, C0 to C7, 3]); in transcriptomics its mean was 2.924 where the true number is 1 [S3, C0, 3, `mean_k`].

**8. Extra-hard scenarios (Figure S8; partial: transcriptomics and lipidomics/metabolomics only, 500 data sets each).** In transcriptomics the recommended design's mean false discovery proportion was 0.035 to 0.052 in the seven judged extra-hard scenarios scored so far [S3/S2 and S3/S3, 7], and it kept its power with three factors (0.762 [S3/S4, 7]; ceiling 0.763 [S3/S4, 2]), where fixing k at 1 gave a power of 0.000 [S3/S4, 11]. The legacy design reached 0.724 with the factor strongly correlated with the groups [S3/S3, 3]. In lipidomics/metabolomics the recommended design was liberal with the factor strongly correlated with the groups (0.130 against the ceiling's 0.072 [S4/S3, 7 and 2]) and with a known unbalanced batch (0.139 against 0.062 [S4/S8, 7 and 2]), and was between 0.037 and 0.066 in the other judged scenarios [S4/S4 and S4/S5 or S4/S6, 7]. These rows are marked `state` = partial in the table and are to be re-read when all four omics are scored.

### Limits (to state in the text)

- **Simulation only.** The simulators generate the linear model that RUV assumes: a failure under it is strong evidence against a design, a pass is weaker evidence for it. No real-data check is reported here.
- **Technical replicates were judged in one scenario,** in the two proteomics routes. In transcriptomics and lipidomics/metabolomics that scenario is void: the ceiling's type I error rate was 0.179 [S3, C6, 2] and 0.135 [S4, C6, 2].
- **Lipidomics/metabolomics has no global-null check.** One false discovery proportion is scored per data set over two assays that each control their own false discovery rate, so the ceiling's mean is 0.130 under the global null [S4, C4, 2] and the scenario is void.
- **One DIA engine.** The DIA route was measured on the `fastlimpadpc` engine; the result is expected, not measured, to hold for the `limpa` engine.
- **Extra-hard scenarios are partial** (two of four omics), and S6 and S7 cover the main scenarios only.
- **The global-null scenario cannot be passed on rule 4** by any design; verdicts are given under both readings.
- **Two combinations offered in the software have no measured figure yet:** a supplied control list with the separation rule's k, and first-pass nulls with the estimated count.

## (c) Relation to the method papers

Only sources that the design review (`kit-124/design-review-20261118.md`) records as read are cited in this section; quotes are as recorded there.

The computations of the package equal those of the reference packages (reference-match tests, not part of this supplement). What differs between designs is how RUV is used.

- **Groups as replicate sets** follow published usage: the RUVSeq vignette's RUVs example forms the replicate sets from the treatment groups (`differences <- makeGroups(x)`), Molania et al. (2019) describe "an approach using pseudo-replicates when technical replicates are not available", and Salim et al. (2022) allow that "cells with the same level of the biological factor of interest can be declared to be a pseudo-replicate set". The legacy design uses the same replicate sets; this is not one of its departures.
- **All features as controls (recommended design)** follows the RUVSeq vignette for replicate-based RUV: "RUVs is robust to that choice and ... using all the genes works well in practice". Molania et al. (2019) put it as an open question for RUV-III ("Does using all genes as negative controls help? We will see examples where doing so does help, and others where it does not"), and Kim et al. (2021) found "no significant differences" between data-driven non-changing controls and all metabolites as controls. A-priori known controls remain the first choice in these papers; the recommended design is the documented fallback when none exist.
- **Factors in the model of the test (recommended and conditional designs)** follow the RUVSeq vignette ("It is important that subsequent DE analysis be done on the original counts ... as removing the unwanted factors from the counts can also remove part of a factor of interest") and the limma documentation for `removeBatchEffect` ("This function is not intended to be used prior to linear modelling"); Nygaard, Rødland and Hovig (2016) show that correcting and then testing "may lead to exaggerated confidence in downstream analyses".
- **A supplied control list** is the usage of the RUV-III-C paper (Poulos et al. 2020: negative controls known a priori) and of Salim et al. (2022: housekeeping genes).
- **First-pass null controls with most features kept** follow the RUVSeq vignette's RUVg example ("least significantly DE genes based on a first-pass DE analysis"; its code keeps all but the top 5,000 genes).
- **Technical replicates as the replicate structure** are the usage of Molania et al. (2019) and Poulos et al. (2020).

**Where the legacy design departs.** (1) Controls: the least significant 1 to 30% of features of a first test, with the share chosen by a sweep; the papers above use either all features, a list known in advance, or a wide first-pass set. (2) k: chosen by the same sweep. (3) The test: in classic proteomics, lipidomics and metabolomics it is run on the corrected data. Departure (3) is itself a published usage: Molania et al. (2019) and Jiang, Gagnon-Bartsch and Speed (arXiv 2310.01918) run limma on the RUV-III-adjusted matrix, the latter stating that "the asymptotic theory developed in this paper does not address the validity of the p-values". The simulations isolate (1) with (2) and (3) separately (finding 4).

**The two rules for k.** The method papers give no rule: the RUVSeq vignette's example uses k = 1, Molania et al. (2019) give no clear rule, and the help page of `ruv::getK` says that it "will not be the best choice in all cases". The *estimated count* used here is the permutation estimate of the number of surrogate variables (`sva::num.sv`, method "be"): it is the convention of surrogate variable analysis applied to RUV, with precedent in applied use only; no RUV method paper proposes it. The *separation rule* (the k at which the canonical-correlation curve of all features and that of the controls are furthest apart, on the plot of `ruv::ruv_cancorplot`, within a bound set by sample size) is the package's own heuristic; no paper was found that proposes it, and it is undefined when all features are the controls. Both statements should stand in the paper as plainly as here.
