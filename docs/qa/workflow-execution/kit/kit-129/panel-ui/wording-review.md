## Glossary (one term per idea, in all four omics)

| Term used | Means | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| unwanted variation | variation between samples that comes from the measurement, not the biology (batch, run order, sample handling); defined in the section's first line | unwanted factor (as the thing present), drift (kept once, for lipid and metab) |
| factor; number of factors (k) | one separate pattern of unwanted variation that is estimated and removed | component, W |
| approach | one of the six or seven ways of removing unwanted variation (a row of the list) | design, preset, option |
| scenario | one simulated situation | design tested, cell |
| reference scenario | 6 samples per group, 20% of features truly different, one unwanted factor; defined in the help line | the standard design, the centre |
| worst scenario tested | the scenario with real differences in which the approach had most false discoveries | worst design tested |
| false discoveries | features reported as different that are not; the figure is their average share at a 5% FDR threshold | false discovery proportion, FDP |
| target (5%) | the false discovery rate the threshold aims for | nominal 5% |
| power | the share of real differences found, shown as a percentage | power as a proportion (0.91) |
| tied to the groups | the unwanted variation differs between the groups (for example most of one group in one batch); with the correlation where one was simulated | follows the groups, correlated with the groups, confounded |
| too many false discoveries | more than the target | liberal |
| false discoveries stay near the 5% target | | calibrated |
| as well as an analysis that was given the true unwanted factor | the best any approach could do in the simulation | matches the known-factor ceiling |
| acceptance criterion; acceptance limit | a pass criterion of the simulation study | rule (in "passed every rule"), limit |
| rule | only a rule for choosing k: estimated number, separation rule, your own number | k rule; estimated count; a number |
| controls; control proteins (genes, lipids, metabolites) | features assumed not to differ between the groups; "negative controls" given once in each description | negative controls |
| replicates | samples treated as repeats of each other; "replicate sets" kept in the tooltip only | replicate sets |
| simulated data sets | the repeated simulations of one scenario | simulated runs |
| plots | pictures | figures (which now means measured numbers only) |
| approach of earlier versions | the tier "Legacy" in words | today's design, the notebook's design, the limpa script's control rule |
| features | proteins, genes, lipids or metabolites, in the texts shared by the four omics | |

## Where plain and exact pulled apart, and what was chosen

1. **"As well as an analysis that was given the true unwanted factor"** for "matches the known-factor ceiling". Exact about what the comparison is; it does not say on which measures (false discoveries and power). Chosen over a longer sentence.
2. **"False discoveries stay near the 5% target"** for "calibrated". "Calibrated" was a statement about the test's error rate as well; "near" covers the 4.4% to 5.9% of the four tables but is looser than the study's criteria. The measured worst figure is beside it in every row.
3. **"5% FDR threshold"** for "nominal 5%". Assumes the reader knows FDR (the brief says they do); the help line spells it out once. "Target 5%" in the heading is shorter and slightly looser.
4. **Power as a percentage** (91%, 0.5%). Easier beside "the share of real differences found", but two percentage columns sit side by side; the headings and the bold figures keep them apart. Statisticians will expect 0.91.
5. **"Tied to the groups"** with the gloss "differs between the groups, for example when most of one group was run in the same batch". The simulation's quantity is a correlation of 0.3 or 0.6 between the factor and the group indicator; the number is kept wherever it was stated, the gloss is an illustration, not the definition.
6. **"Patterns (principal components)" and "random shuffles (permutations)"** in the estimated number's explanation. The plain word leads and the exact term is in brackets; "explain more of the variation than they do in 20 random shuffles" leaves out that the comparison is made at a fixed significance level (0.10), which the old text also left out.
7. **"The first k patterns in the data are related to the groups"** for the canonical-correlation plot. Exact enough for the reader who opens the plot; it does not say "squared first canonical correlation".
8. **"Acceptance criterion" / "acceptance limit"** without saying what the criteria are. They are the simulation study's pass rules and are not listed in the panel; a pointer to the paper's Methods would be the complete answer once it exists.
9. **When-to-use leads that the old notes did not have**: "The general-purpose choice" (recommended), "Use when the experimental design declares technical replicates", "Use when you have proteins known not to differ between the groups", "Only when little unwanted variation is expected" (none; not in DIA with limpa, where it fails), "For reproducing analyses made with earlier versions" (added to DIA with limpa and transcriptomics from decision M3; not to lipid and metab, decision M8). These are guidance drawn from the tier and the measured figures, not new measurements: the owner should confirm each.
