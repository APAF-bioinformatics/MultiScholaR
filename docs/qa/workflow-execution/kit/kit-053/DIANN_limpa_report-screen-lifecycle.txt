Data import, filtering and quantification

Precursor-level identifications and quantities were imported from the
DIA-NN main report (one row per precursor and run). Low-confidence
peptide identifications were removed using q-value and proteotypic
peptide filtering, retaining only peptides with a q-value threshold of
0.01 and only those that are proteotypic. Precursor ion intensities were
then aggregated into peptide-level quantification values, combining all
charge states of the same peptide sequence while keeping different
modified peptides (peptidoforms) as separate entries. The peptide
quantities were arranged as a peptide-by-sample matrix and log2
transformed (values at or below zero treated as missing); one protein
accession was chosen per protein group from the FASTA metadata and
duplicate peptides of a resolved group were aggregated.

Protein-level quantification was performed with the limpa package’s
Detection Probability Curve method, DPC-Quant (Li et al., 2025), on the
‘limpa’ engine (version 1.4.2). Unlike roll-up approaches that first
aggregate peptides to proteins and then impute missing values, DPC-Quant
quantifies proteins and handles missing values in one statistical model:
a detection probability curve fitted on the peptide matrix (fitted
intercept 0.264, slope 0.012: nearly random missing) relates peptide
intensity to the probability of detection, and maximum posterior
estimates with standard errors are returned for every protein in every
sample. Proteins were required to have at least 1 unique peptide(s) and
2 peptidoform(s) to be considered for further analysis. No protein-level
missing-value filter applies: the quantified matrix is complete. No
between-sample normalisation was applied to the quantified log2 matrix
(the limpa workflow’s default).

To account for unwanted variation, RUV-III (Molania et al., 2019) was
fitted on the quantified protein matrix using a set of endogenous
negative control proteins chosen by a negative-control sweep (4% of
proteins, 100 controls; replicate structure: group). The 1 estimated
factor(s) of unwanted variation (W) were appended to the design as
covariates of the differential abundance model; the protein matrix
itself was not corrected (the RUV-adjusted matrix is used for
quality-control plots only).

At each stage, the samples were checked for batch effects using
principal component analysis (PCA) plots, density boxplots, relative
log-expression (RLE) plots and the distribution of the Pearson
correlation between replicate samples within groups; samples whose
correlation with their group fell below the configured threshold were
removed.

Differential Abundance Analysis

DIFFERENTIAL ABUNDANCE ANALYSIS

Differential abundance analysis of proteins was performed on the
DPC-quantified protein abundances for the comparisons defined in the
contrast table. The limpa R package’s ‘dpcDE’ function (Li et al., 2025)
fitted a linear model using the formula ‘~ 0 + group + W1’, weighting
each observation by the uncertainty of its DPC-Quant estimate (imputed
values down-weighted), with the ‘limma’ R package (Ritchie et al., 2015)
underneath. The 1 RUV-III factor(s) of unwanted variation (W) entered
the model as covariates. Contrasts were applied with ‘contrasts.fit’ and
p-values were calculated using the ‘eBayes’ function. A trended and
robust empirical Bayes analysis was performed. The false discovery rate
correction was applied to the moderated p-values by calculating the
q-values (Storey, 2002). No log-fold-change threshold (TREAT) applies on
this model. Significant differentially expressed proteins were defined
as those with q-values less than 0.05.

Volcano plots of differentially expressed proteins across all groups are
shown in Figure 2, with a threshold of q-value < 0.05 and no log
fold-change threshold. Full details of all proteins are provided in the
supplementary results table DA_results_proteomics.xlsx within the
Publication_tables folder.
