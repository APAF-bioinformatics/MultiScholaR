## 1. The question

The arm without RUV (NO), applied to the planted-anomaly data sets of tier D, with the correlation filter at 0.5 read BEFORE RUV, failed the planted sample in 184, 135 and 45 of 200 data sets at 3, 4 and 6 samples per group (VAL-384), not in about every data set as the control assumed.
1. **Does switching the between-sample normalisation restore the filter's failure of the planted sample?** The same data sets are run under the shipped default (cyclic loess, as `runWorkflowOmicsReferenceBetweenSampleNormalisation` calls it with `"cyclicloess"`: `limma::normalizeCyclicLoess` at limma's defaults, method "fast"), quantile (`limma::normalizeQuantiles`) and none.
2. **Does the planted sample's best within-group correlation rise with group size under cyclic loess?**

## 6. Readings declared in advance (diagnostic; nothing is decided; the auditor's RL3 of 2026-11-26)

The numbers below are indicative anchors from RL3's linear stand-in (planted sample's correlation about 0.44, 0.51, 0.57 at 6, 8, 12 samples) and from KIT-255 (-0.015 under quantile), not predictions that can fail; no threshold turns a reading into a decision.
- **R-pull (normalisation's pull toward the average).** Under cyclic loess the planted sample's best correlation lies near 0.5 and its median rises from D3 to D6, the count below 0.5 falling with group size (indicatively about 40 to 50 of 50 at D3, about 20 to 40 at D4, about 5 to 25 at D6, in line with 0.92, 0.675, 0.225); **under quantile and under none** the values lie near 0 and about every data set is below 0.5 (a count of 47 or more of 50, lower 95 % limit about 0.80 or above, at all three designs). This supports RL3's mechanism for the whole of the shortfall and shows that switching the normalisation restores the failure.
- **R-plant (a plant issue).** Values near the genuine sample's (about 0.9) under all three normalisations, including none and quantile, would point at the plant or the data rather than the normalisation, although E1 to E3 held.
- **R-mixture (chance exceptions, not a pull).** Under cyclic loess values near 0 in most data sets with a few high ones, and the same under quantile and none, would mean the shortfall comes from something the normalisation does not change (the drift's share, the maximum over mates), not from the pull.
- **R-partial.** Under quantile or none a count clearly below 50 (a lower limit below 0.80) means normalisation is not the whole cause; the diagnostic cannot divide the remainder among the loess pull, the drift term and the maximum over mates (RL3's "not established" list).
- **Growth with group size.** A rising planted median from D3 to D6 under cyclic loess, with no rise under quantile or none, is the pattern RL3 expects; a flat median is not.
- The genuine sample is a comparison, not a reference at 0.9: at D3 the generator's drift can put a genuine sample near 0.5 (VAL-384, 7.4, NC1's note).
Also reported, not read: `e5_y` and `n_removed_y` against `n_fail_y`; the status counts.
